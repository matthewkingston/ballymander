# Voting model

Work towards a **voting mode** for the app: estimate how many voters of each
party live in every Data Zone, so elections can be simulated in any drawn
constituency and the map can be gerrymandered by votes as it already is by
demographics. It will never match real polling. It is a tool for thinking about
voting systems and boundaries, and the aim is "decently plausible".

This document records where the model stands at the **voters v0 checkpoint**
(prior v2, transfer matrix v0, and the fit to three elections): what is built, the evidence behind each choice, and, kept separate, what is
still open. Nothing here touches the app yet; the demographics mode and
`build/dz_attributes.csv` are unchanged.

Every decision below was made explicitly by the project owner. Choices made
during implementation that were flagged but not separately discussed are
labelled **implementation choice**. Anything under
[Open questions](#open-questions-and-proposals) is **not decided**.

## Inputs

* **Elections: first preferences as the measure of intention.** 2023 council
  (80 DEAs), 2022 Assembly (18 constituencies, 2008 boundaries), 2024
  Westminster (18, 2024 boundaries). Who was elected is deliberately unused:
  first preferences are the only fully transparent measure of intention.
  The prior uses the council layer only. The other two enter at the calibration
  stage.
* **STV transfers, for the transfer matrix only.** Where a party's voters go
  when it isn't on the ballot is estimated from the stage-by-stage transfers on
  the Assembly and council count sheets. They are never used as a measure of
  intention.
* **Party assignments** follow [Voting modelling
  assumptions](../README.md#voting-modelling-assumptions) in the README (for
  example, Alex Easton counted as DUP).
* **Electorate per DZ** (`data/dz21_electorate.json`): the DEA register shared
  out by adult population.
* **Census 2021**: religion brought up in, current religion (DZ); detailed
  religion (SDZ only); social grade; national identity.

## Prior v2

A demographic estimate of each party's share in every DZ. It is the starting
point that election results will later adjust.

**v2 vs v1:** v1 handled parties that didn't stand by masking. Their voters
were spread over the parties that did stand in proportion to those parties'
shares, so DUP and UUP absorbed TUV support by size. v2 sends them along
[transfer matrix v0](#transfer-matrix-v0) instead, and the penalties, tau and
covariance settings were re-tuned. v1 is in git history (commit 706ec5c).

**Parties modelled (9):** Sinn Féin, DUP, Alliance, UUP, SDLP, TUV, Green, PBP,
Aontú. **Independents are transparent**: dropped from both sides, so their
voters are assumed to behave like the average voter of their area in another
election. **Micro-parties are transparent too** for now: PUP, Conservative,
IRSP, Workers Party, CCLA, Socialist Party. Revisiting that, possibly bloc-aware,
is flagged for later.

**Features (24), all population shares** (definitions in
`scripts/build_model_features.py`):

| Group | Features |
|---|---|
| Religion brought up in | Catholic; Protestant & Other Christian; None + Other religions |
| Current religion | lapsed Catholic; lapsed Protestant; Presbyterian; Church of Ireland; Methodist; remaining Other Christian |
| SDZ religion detail | unspecified Protestant ("Protestant", "Protestant (Mixed)"); conservative pool (Reformed Presbyterian + Pentecostal); Free Presbyterian |
| Social grade | AB; C1; C2; DE (shares of the graded population) |
| National identity | all 8 categories |

* SDZ counts are shared out across that SDZ's DZs in proportion to their
  "Other Christian" count. "Remaining Other Christian" is what's left, so no
  religion feature is nested inside another.
* Lapsed shares are left unclipped. They dip just below zero in a few DZs,
  where the two tables' disclosure noise disagrees.
* Age bands were tested and dropped.

**Model:**

* **Joint multinomial logit** across the nine parties.
  * **Symmetric**: every party has coefficients, and every category of every
    group is included. There is no reference party or category.
  * DZ shares always cover all nine parties: every party's voters, whether or
    not it stood.
* **Mapped onto each DEA's ballot through the transfer matrix.** A party that
  stood keeps its voters. An absent party's voters go to the parties that stood,
  in proportion to its transfer-matrix row renormalised over them. That is a
  **direct renormalisation**, not a cascade through other absent parties,
  matching how the matrix was estimated (see caveats).
* **Fitted at DZ level against DEA results.** Each DZ is predicted from its own
  features, summed to its DEA weighted by electorate (turnout flat within a
  DEA), put through the DEA's ballot, and scored against the DEA's council
  result (vote-weighted log-likelihood). Fitting at the level the model is applied at removes the
  aggregation bias that flattens a DEA-fitted model.
* **Features standardised.** At prediction time they are clipped to the
  training range extended by **0.5 of that range each side**. This guard was
  decided when the prior was fitted on DEAs and applied to the more extreme
  DZs: against the DEA range it clipped about 22% of DZs. Since the prior trains
  on DZs, the range already covers every DZ. The final fit clips none, and
  cross-validation clips 1 of 3,780 held-out DZs. **It is now effectively
  inactive**, and only matters if the model is applied to data outside
  training.
* **Bloc-structured ridge penalty.**
  * Within-bloc penalty: each party's deviation from its bloc's mean
    coefficients.
  * Bloc penalty: the size of the bloc means.
  * Blocs: nationalist = SF, SDLP, PBP, Aontú; unionist = DUP, UUP, TUV;
    other = Alliance, Green. PBP and Aontú are placed with the nationalists for
    now, knowing the fit isn't perfect.
  * Intercepts unpenalised.
* **Penalties by cross-validation minimum** (leave one council out, 11 folds):
  **bloc 0.1, within-bloc 3.16**, an interior minimum.
  * Grid: bloc 0.001–1, within-bloc 0.1–10. Every fit starts from zero, and a
    check at 31.6 and 100 was worse.
  * The curve near the minimum:

    | within-bloc | 1 | 3.16 | 10 | 31.6 |
    |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
    | KL | 53.5 | **52.2** | 52.9 | 54.5 |

  * Earlier curves reused each fit as the next one's starting point. Under v2
    that left some fits short of their optimum and hid this minimum. Full-data
    fits reach the same solution from any start.
* **Slope correction tau = 1.1.** All slopes are scaled by 1.1, then the
  intercepts are refitted. This trades about 0.6 mnats of DEA-level fit for
  realistic segregation extremes, which DEA-level validation cannot see.
  * The transfer mapping alone removed much of v1's over-mixing, so tau came
    down from v1's 1.2.

    | tau | 1.0 | 1.1 | 1.2 |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | KL | 52.7 | 53.3 | 55.0 |
    | DZ extremes | 3.0% / 3.7% | **2.2% / 2.7%** | 1.6% / 2.0% |

    (DZ extremes are defined in [Evidence](#evidence).)
* **Output shares are unmasked** (all nine parties present). The prior
  describes voters, not a ballot paper.

## Prior covariance

How the prior's errors vary and covary between parties. It is used to **weight
the prior against election evidence** in the calibration fit, not as a final
confidence interval. All steps are in `scripts/prior_covariance.py`.

1. **Out-of-sample errors.** Refit the prior with each council held out, and
   predict its DEAs through each DEA's ballot.
2. **Log-ratio representation.** Errors are proportional, so small parties are
   not pushed toward zero too cheaply. Shares are floored at 0.2%.
   *Implementation choice:* the floor value, DEAs unweighted.
3. **Variation matrix.**
   * For each party pair, take the variance of (predicted − actual)
     log(p/q), over DEAs where both stood.
   * Convert to a clr covariance: S = −½ HTH.
   * Pairwise log-ratios need no common set of parties. The first attempt
     centred over the parties that stood in each DEA, which produced an invalid
     matrix (min eigenvalue −0.88).
4. **One global matrix** for all areas; area-varying uncertainty is a
   follow-up.
5. **Shrinkage toward bloc-structured targets.**
   * Each correlation is pulled toward the overlap-weighted mean of its pair
     type, by λ = k/(k+n), where n = DEAs where both stood.
   * **Six pair types**: within each bloc, and three separate cross-bloc
     types, since e.g. SDLP–Green shouldn't be forced to resemble SF–TUV.
   * **k = 10 for nationalist–other pairs, 60 for all others.** These are
     rounded from a council-block bootstrap estimate on prior v2 (noise
     c = 1.96):
     * nationalist–other k ≈ 11;
     * unionist–other ≈ 77;
     * pooled over all types ≈ 59;
     * within-bloc and nationalist–unionist: no spread beyond noise.

     v1's estimates had given 20 and 200.
   * *Implementation choice:* the bootstrap as the noise estimate.
6. **Repair.** One eigenvalue came out at −0.006 and was **clipped to zero**.
   The largest change to any correlation is 0.013, against 0.0015 for v1.
7. **Scaling to larger regions (Method B).** Keep the DEA-scale correlations
   and scale the variances by (region population / DEA population)^β, with
   **common β = −0.35**. This is recorded in the output and applied downstream.
   At constituency size (~4.6× a DEA) the variance factor is 0.59.
   * Measured on prior v2: class means −0.35, per party −0.25 (Green) to
     −0.48 (UUP). v1's measurement gave −0.37, which was then set to −0.4.
   * Method A was rejected: it would build the parliamentary layers'
     uncertainty from council results that are then combined with those same
     layers.

Resulting DEA-scale values:

| | SF | DUP | Alliance | UUP | SDLP | TUV | Green | PBP | Aontú |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| error SD (log-ratio) | 0.30 | 0.32 | 0.40 | 0.43 | 0.63 | 0.38 | 0.61 | 0.63 | 0.42 |

| Pair type | within nationalist | within unionist | within other | nationalist–unionist | nationalist–other | unionist–other |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| target correlation | −0.20 | −0.20 | −0.38 | +0.04 | −0.12 | +0.07 |

A 9-party clr matrix averages −0.125 by construction. Within-bloc pairs now
show a modest trade-off beyond that baseline (−0.20). Nationalist–other stays
negative and unionist–other positive.

## Transfer matrix v0

Where a party's voters go when that party isn't standing. Calibration needs it
in reverse: in a DEA without TUV, some TUV voters are already inside the DUP
and UUP totals. Simulation needs it forwards. v0 was built with a
minimum-viable-product mindset: data-based, but simple.

**Data** (`scripts/parse_transfers.py`):

* Stage-by-stage transfers from all 18 Assembly workbooks and 73 of 80 council
  DEAs. That is every council xlsx and text PDF; the 7 scanned Fermanagh &
  Omagh sheets are excluded.
* An **event** is one stage with a single source candidate: an elimination, or
  the transfer of a surplus.
* Skipped:
  * stages where several candidates were excluded together (46), since their
    flows can't be split;
  * stages that don't balance (15). These are mostly undistributed final
    eliminations; Belfast East's stage 11 is an error on the sheet itself.
* Three council PDFs (Crotlieve, Downpatrick, Slieve Gullion) never print their
  final stages.
* Parsing checks:
  * PDF columns are accepted only if every printed running total chains.
  * Surplus vs elimination is decided by whether the source had reached quota.
    This agrees with the sheet's own label at all 332 labelled stages.
* Parties, including the independents counted as parties, come from the
  first-preference JSONs. Independents and micro-parties are transparent:
  transfers from them are ignored, and transfers to them are dropped with the
  rest renormalised.

**Clean events** (`scripts/fit_transfer_matrix.py`):

* **No running mate still in the count.** Otherwise same-party transfers swamp
  the cross-party signal.
* **At most 25% of what's passed on came from other parties' voters.**
  First preferences and transfers from a running mate count as the party's own.
  For a surplus, only the last parcel received is examined, as in NI counts,
  so the test applies to that parcel.
* Result: 221 clean events from 506 single-source events.

  | From | SF | TUV | UUP | Alliance | SDLP | DUP | Green | Aontú | PBP |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | clean events | 48 | 35 | 32 | 28 | 24 | 18 | 18 | 11 | 7 |

**Model:**

* **Non-transferable votes are renormalised away**, consistent with flat
  turnout. Aontú's transfers are 36% non-transferable, the highest.
* **Availability-adjusted.**
  * Each source party has one weight per destination party.
  * In an event, a destination's share is its weight divided by the total
    weight of the parties still in the count. Weights are fitted by the standard
    minorise-maximise update.
  * The matrix is applied the same way: renormalise a row over the parties
    standing. So it is still proportional *within* whoever stands, but with
    party-specific rates rather than first-preference sizes.
  * Pooled percentages were visibly wrong without this. For example,
    Alliance→Green was 4% because Green was rarely still in the count.
* **Every event counts once**, whatever its size. Weighting by votes gave
  similar results.
* **Shrinkage toward bloc expectations.**
  * Each row gets **k = 5** pseudo-events in which all eight parties are
    available, split by target shares. Pairs seen in few events end up near the
    target; well-observed pairs follow their data.
  * Targets: the same fit pooled over every source party in the bloc, with the
    party itself left out and the rest renormalised. For example, SDLP's target
    uses the pooled SF, SDLP, PBP and Aontú events.
  * **No all-party fallback.** Targets are bloc expectations, so DUP voters'
    behaviour must not inform Alliance→Green.
  * **Alliance→Green and Green→Alliance targets are hand-set at 35%**, as a
    placeholder. The "other" bloc has no bloc-mates to pool.

**Result**: % of a party's transferring voters going to each other party if
all eight stood (rows sum to 100).

| From \ to | SF | DUP | All | UUP | SDLP | TUV | Grn | PBP | Aon |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **SF** | — | 0 | 16 | 1 | 53 | 0 | 9 | 9 | 12 |
| **DUP** | 0 | — | 4 | 55 | 2 | 35 | 3 | 1 | 0 |
| **Alliance** | 7 | 2 | — | 11 | 28 | 1 | 32 | 15 | 4 |
| **UUP** | 0 | 44 | 18 | — | 5 | 24 | 6 | 2 | 1 |
| **SDLP** | 19 | 1 | 31 | 3 | — | 1 | 19 | 11 | 16 |
| **TUV** | 0 | 62 | 3 | 29 | 2 | — | 3 | 1 | 0 |
| **Green** | 10 | 2 | 35 | 5 | 20 | 1 | — | 25 | 4 |
| **PBP** | 11 | 1 | 20 | 2 | 32 | 1 | 22 | — | 11 |
| **Aontú** | 27 | 4 | 9 | 1 | 35 | 2 | 11 | 11 | — |

* **Driven entirely by the target:** SDLP→Aontú and PBP→Aontú have no events
  at all. SDLP→Aontú at 16% comes from SF-dominated nationalist pooling and may
  be high.
* **Most affected by shrinkage:** PBP (7 events) and Aontú (11). Unshrunk,
  PBP→Green is 38% (22% shrunk) and Aontú→SF 37% (27%).
* **Sensitivity to k:** from k = 2 to 10, thin pairs move by up to 8 points,
  and well-observed pairs by 1–3.
* **Pooling side-effect:** each unionist row is pulled slightly toward the
  bloc's combined pattern, e.g. UUP→DUP 40% unshrunk, 44% shrunk.

## Fitting (voters v0)

Combines prior v2 with the three elections into one estimate of voter shares
per DZ (`scripts/fit_voters.py`). The layers are the 2023 council (80 DEAs),
the 2022 Assembly (18 constituencies, 2008 boundaries) and 2024 Westminster
(18 constituencies, 2024 boundaries). Each DZ belongs to one region per layer;
DZs split by a constituency boundary go to the constituency holding most of
them.

**1. One shift per region.**
* In each layer, each region gets one log-ratio shift `a_r`, applied to all of
  its DZs: `x_z ∝ p_z · exp(a_r)`. The region's result sets the overall level,
  and demographics still spread it across the region's DZs.
* **Prior term:** `a_r ~ N(0, Σ_r)`, where Σ_r is the prior covariance scaled by
  (region population / average DEA population)^−0.35.
* **Prediction:** the electorate-weighted sum of the region's DZ shares, put
  through the region's ballot with the transfer matrix.
* **Absent parties** need no special case. The ballot mapping shows where their
  voters went, and one direction per absent party (that party against its own
  transfer mix) is left to the prior and to other layers.
* Rejected: separate adjustments per DZ, which would need correlations of
  prior errors between DZs that we don't have.

**2. Data term: soft, in log-ratios.**
* The observed log-ratios among parties that stood are Gaussian around the
  prediction, with variance = a per-layer floor + counting noise. A plain
  multinomial was rejected: thousands of votes would force an exact fit.
* The floor stands for everything a result doesn't share with true voter
  preferences: election type, candidates, local campaigns, swing between
  elections. It is the same for every party pair, and scaled to region size
  like the prior.
* Floors, as pairwise log-ratio SDs at constituency scale: **council 0.3,
  Assembly 0.2, Westminster 0.55**. The council floor is about 0.39 at a
  typical DEA.
* The floors were measured from how much real results disagree across layers.
  Each layer's votes were spread to DZs by the prior's pattern, summed onto
  the other layer's constituencies, and compared for party pairs standing in
  both. Counting noise was removed.

  | Layer pair | Log-ratio RMS disagreement |
  |---|---:|
  | council vs Assembly | 0.34 |
  | council vs Westminster | 0.61 |
  | Assembly vs Westminster | 0.57 |

  Solving the three equations gives floors of 0.29 / 0.18 / 0.54. Systematic
  offsets between election types are **included**: the election-type factor is
  deferred, so they are unexplained error for now.
* Westminster's large floor is plausible given FPTP tactical voting,
  stand-asides and fewer candidates.

**3. Each layer's uncertainty from curvature.**
`C_r = (JᵀΩ⁻¹J + Σ_r⁻¹)⁻¹` at the fitted shift, where J is how the predicted
log-ratios respond to the shift. This is the principled version of perturbing
pairs of parties. A layer is confident in the directions its result observes
and falls back to the prior's uncertainty in the directions it can't see.

**4. Combining the layers.**
* Each layer's **data contribution** `D = JᵀΩ⁻¹J` is combined, not its whole
  fit. Otherwise the prior would be counted once per layer and pull the
  estimate back toward itself.
* **Within-region spread.** A region's shift applies to a given DZ only
  approximately, because local areas differ from their region in ways
  demographics miss. That is where the earlier 1/sqrt(pop) weighting went: a
  variance term, not a multiplier.
  * `H_r = h² · (region population / constituency population)^0.5`, with
    **h = 0.6**, the same for every party pair.
  * It inflates each data contribution in the directions it observes:
    `D̃ = (D⁻¹ + H_r)⁻¹`.
  * h was measured as the spread of council DEAs' out-of-sample residuals
    around their constituency's residual: 0.59 (2008 boundaries) / 0.63 (2024
    boundaries), after removing the council floor's local part. Both are
    probably overestimates.
* **Per DZ,** on top of its DEA's prior:
  `C_z⁻¹ = Σ_DEA⁻¹ + Σ_L D̃_L`, `m_z = C_z Σ_L D̃_L u_L`, and shares
  `x_z ∝ p_z exp(m_z)`.
  * At a typical DZ, the observed-direction weights come out roughly council 1
    : Assembly 0.8 : Westminster 0.5.
* Everything is computed in the 8 free log-ratio dimensions.

**Results:**

RMS pairwise log-ratio error on each layer's own ballots:

| Layer | Prior | That layer's own fit | Combined estimate | Floor |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| council 2023 | 0.53 | 0.21 | 0.36 | 0.3 (0.39 at DEA) |
| Assembly 2022 | 0.80 | 0.16 | 0.36 | 0.2 |
| Westminster 2024 | 0.79 | 0.54 | 0.59 | 0.55 |

By design, the combined estimate reproduces no single election exactly; it
sits between them.

NI-wide voter shares, prior → combined:

| | SF | DUP | Alliance | UUP | SDLP | TUV | Green | PBP | Aontú |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Prior | 30.6 | 24.6 | 13.2 | 11.4 | 7.8 | 5.7 | 2.1 | 2.6 | 1.9 |
| Combined | 30.3 | 23.3 | 14.5 | 11.0 | 9.3 | 6.1 | 1.9 | 1.8 | 1.7 |

SDLP 0.26, TUV 0.22, Green 0.30, PBP 0.35, Aontú 0.28.

Example: Airport DEA had no TUV candidate.
* Its council fit alone infers 4.0% TUV voters from where transfers went.
* Combined with the South Antrim Assembly result (TUV 9.6%), the estimate is
  4.9%.

**Checks:**
* With the council layer alone and no spread term, every DZ's combined shift
  equals its DEA's own fitted shift (to 1e-6). This confirms prior and data
  contributions are separated correctly.
* A fit observes exactly (parties standing − 1) directions.
* The script stops if any region's fit fails to converge.
* The prior covariance is well conditioned in the 8 free dimensions (condition
  number 31).

## Evidence

Exploratory tests, run in September 2026 and kept here as results; their
scripts are not committed.

* **KL** = vote-weighted KL divergence per vote, in millinats, out of sample
  (leave one council out); lower is better.
* **DZ extremes** = median prior unionist share in DZs >95% Catholic-raised /
  nationalist share in DZs >90% Protestant-raised. For reference, the most
  extreme real DEAs where both blocs stood: Newry (88% Catholic) 2.8% unionist;
  Ballyclare (5% Catholic) 1.4% nationalist.

Nested CV, ridge logit fitted on DEAs, pooled "Other" category (before
micro-parties were made transparent).

|---|---:|
| intercepts only | 177.7 |
| upbringing only | 78.0 |
| + current religion | 75.2 |
| + SDZ detail | 80.0 |
| + grade | 78.0 |
| + age | 106.5 |
| + identity | 70.2 |
| all 28 | 70.4 |

Unique cost of removing each group from all 28:

| Group removed | KL cost |
|---|---:|
| identity | +4.3 ± 1.4 |
| current religion | +3.4 ± 1.4 |
| grade | +3.0 ± 1.4 |
| upbringing | +2.2 ± 0.7 |
| SDZ detail | +1.3 ± 0.8 |
| **age** | **+0.2 ± 0.3 → dropped** |

Age also broke under extrapolation: Botanic, 50% aged 18–29, reached z = 20
when Belfast was held out.

### Individual religion categories

Weighted partial correlations, with drop-top-N and jackknife checks.

* **Unspecified Protestant** is the only variable explaining DUP's share within
  unionism; it survived both robustness tests.
* **Rejected:** Church of England, a small-denominator artefact from 3 Foyle
  DEAs (+0.61 collapsed to +0.09 on dropping 6), and Non-Subscribing
  Presbyterian (1,098 people, noise).
* **Conservative pool:** Free Presbyterian was taken out of it to avoid
  nesting, leaving Reformed Presbyterian + Pentecostal. Its earlier
  robustness came substantially from Free Presbyterian, and it was not
  re-tested alone.

  calibration, not demographics.**

### Weighting and scaling

* **Layer weight exponent.**
  * Test: council DEAs were merged into contiguous regions, the regions'
    results redistributed by demographics, and the misallocation measured
    against region size.
  * Allocation error variance grows as about pop^0.6. Neither 0.5 nor 1.0 is
    excluded.
  * **1/sqrt(pop)** was chosen as the principled round form.
* **β for prior error.**
  * Regions: single DEAs, adjacent same-council pairs, councils.
  * Metric: pairwise log-ratio error of region totals.
  * Result: class means −0.37, per party −0.32 (Green) to −0.48 (Alliance,
    UUP). A common value is well supported.
  * For comparison, fully independent errors would give −1: errors are
    strongly spatially clustered.

## Assumptions and caveats

* **Intention is fixed across election types.** This is known to be faulty: for
  example, SF and Green run higher at council, and SDLP, UUP and Alliance higher
  at Westminster, though standing confounds all of it.
* **Turnout** varies by election type but is flat across geography, demography
  and party. Layers combine as **shares**, not votes.
* **Absent parties' voters go directly to the parties standing.**
  * Each absent party's transfer-matrix row is renormalised over the parties
    standing. A B voter who would have picked another absent party C is
    treated as a typical B voter, not a C voter.
  * A cascade (B→C→…) would make the opposite assumption. The truth is probably
    in between. **To consider going forward.**
  * Within whoever stands, shares still follow fixed party-specific rates (the
    matrix's proportional form).
* **Selection.** Parties stand where they are strong. A party's prior in areas
  where it never stood is extrapolated from where it did.
* **Religion and identity shares include children.** No religion-by-age
  breakdown exists per DZ.
* **Collinearity.** For example, up_protestant ~ id_british 0.96. Individual
  coefficients are not interpretable, only predictions.
* **Sparse features at DZ level** get amplified by standardisation. Free
  Presbyterian is zero in 27% of DZs.
* **DZ extremes can't be validated.** No results below DEA level are
  published; tau was chosen by judgement against the most extreme real DEAs.
* **Global covariance over-trusts the prior in heartlands.** Example: TUV's
  out-of-sample SD was 1.8 points in 30–55% unionist DEAs vs 7.0 points above
  55%.
* **Local micro-party caveats:** PUP 13.7% in Coleraine, CCLA 6.6% in
  Enniskillen.
* **Electorate within a DEA** follows adult population, so a DEA's registration
  ratio applies uniformly to its DZs.
* **STV transfers are not Westminster votes.** Lower preferences cost a voter
  nothing, so they probably overstate cross-party willingness compared with an
  actual FPTP vote. Non-transferable votes (a proxy for abstaining) are not
  modelled.
* **Transfer events are selective.** Parties are observed transferring mainly
  when eliminated or when a single candidate is elected early, and
  multi-candidate exclusions (often small parties together) are lost.
* **The transfer matrix pools elections and areas.** One matrix covers the
  whole of NI, the Assembly and council together.
* **Council results are used twice.** The prior was fitted to them, and the
  council layer then fits them again. The prior's covariance comes from
  out-of-sample errors, so it overstates the prior's uncertainty at those DEAs,
  which lets council data dominate slightly more.
* **Floors and h are rough.**
  * Each comparison covers only 18 constituencies, and party pairs within a
    constituency aren't independent.
  * The floors mix election-type effects, swing (1–2 years apart) and
    allocation error.
  * h probably overstates the within-region spread.
* **Linearised uncertainty.** Curvature-based uncertainty and combining data
  contributions are exact only for a linear fit. The ballot mapping and
  log-ratios make it mildly non-linear.
* **One shift per region.** Within a DEA, DZs differ only by demographics. The
  spread term widens the uncertainty but doesn't change the point estimate.

## Open questions and proposals

**None of this is decided.**

* **Replacing the 35% Alliance↔Green placeholder** with a considered value.
* **Direct renormalisation vs cascading** transfers through absent parties (see
  caveats).
* **Bloc vs party confidence**: whether the app shows bloc outcomes with more
  confidence than party outcomes. Raised by Claude, not yet discussed.
* **Micro-parties**, possibly bloc-aware now that a transfer matrix exists:
  PUP + Conservative unionist, IRSP + Workers Party republican, CCLA +
  Socialist Party transparent.
* **Three-tier evidence scheme** (Claude's proposal), with Tier B deriving DEA
  support by subtracting council results from constituency totals. The owner
  challenged it: the subtraction concentrates every cross-election mismatch in
  one small area. On real data it gave negative and absurd implied TUV shares
  (−1.4% North Down, 29.7% Strangford). Claude recommends dropping it; not
  formally decided.
* **Sanity checks, not scores to chase** (Claude's proposal): on real boundaries, does the model
  reproduce the 2024 Westminster 18-seat result and the 2022 Assembly result?
