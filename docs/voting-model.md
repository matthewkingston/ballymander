# Voting model

Work towards a **voting mode** for the app: estimate how many voters of each
party live in every Data Zone, so elections can be simulated in any drawn
constituency and the map can be gerrymandered by votes as it already is by
demographics. It will never match real polling. It is a tool for thinking about
voting systems and boundaries, and the aim is "decently plausible".

This document records where the model stands at the **voters v1 checkpoint**
(prior v3, transfer matrix v0, and the fit to three elections): what is built,
the evidence behind each choice, and, kept separate, what is still open. What
of it reaches the map is in [In the app](#in-the-app); the demographic terms
and `build/dz_attributes.csv` are unchanged.

Every decision below was made explicitly by the project owner. Choices made
during implementation that were flagged but not separately discussed are
labelled **implementation choice**. Anything under
[Open questions](#open-questions-and-proposals) is **not decided**.

## Inputs

* **Elections: first preferences as the measure of intention.** 2023 council
  (80 DEAs), 2022 Assembly (18 constituencies, 2008 boundaries), 2024
  Westminster (18, 2024 boundaries). Who was elected is deliberately unused:
  first preferences are the only fully transparent measure of intention.
  The prior uses the council layer only. The other two enter at the calibration
  stage.
* **STV transfers, for the transfer matrix only.** Where a party's voters go
  when it isn't on the ballot is estimated from the stage-by-stage transfers on
  the Assembly and council count sheets. They are never used as a measure of
  intention.
* **Party assignments** follow [Voting modelling
  assumptions](../README.md#voting-modelling-assumptions) in the README (for
  example, Alex Easton counted as DUP).
* **Electorate per DZ** (`data/dz21_electorate.json`): the DEA register shared
  out by adult population.
* **Census 2021**: religion brought up in, current religion (DZ); detailed
  religion (SDZ only); social grade; national identity.

## Prior v3

A demographic estimate of each party's share in every DZ. It is the starting
point that election results will later adjust.

**v3 vs v2:** v2 aggregated Data Zones to a DEA by **electorate**, which says
every elector is equally likely to vote. v3 weights each zone by the votes it
casts — electorate times its [turnout index](#turnout-v0) — so a fitted share
means "of the ballots cast here" rather than "of a place where everyone votes
alike". The weights are normalised inside each DEA, so only turnout differences
*within* a DEA move anything; differences between DEAs were always carried by
fitting each DEA to its own totals. The settled penalties survived the refit
untouched: the CV minimum is still bloc 0.1 / within 3.16.

**v2 vs v1:** v1 handled parties that didn't stand by masking. Their voters
were spread over the parties that did stand in proportion to those parties'
shares, so DUP and UUP absorbed TUV support by size. v2 sends them along
[transfer matrix v0](#transfer-matrix-v0) instead, and the penalties, tau and
covariance settings were re-tuned. v1 is in git history (commit 706ec5c).

**Parties modelled (9):** Sinn Féin, DUP, Alliance, UUP, SDLP, TUV, Green, PBP,
Aontú. **Independents are transparent**: dropped from both sides, so their
voters are assumed to behave like the average voter of their area in another
election. **Micro-parties are transparent too** for now: PUP, Conservative,
IRSP, Workers Party, CCLA, Socialist Party. Revisiting that, possibly bloc-aware,
is flagged for later.

**Features (24), all population shares** (definitions in
`scripts/build_model_features.py`):

| Group | Features |
|---|---|
| Religion brought up in | Catholic; Protestant & Other Christian; None + Other religions |
| Current religion | lapsed Catholic; lapsed Protestant; Presbyterian; Church of Ireland; Methodist; remaining Other Christian |
| SDZ religion detail | unspecified Protestant ("Protestant", "Protestant (Mixed)"); conservative pool (Reformed Presbyterian + Pentecostal); Free Presbyterian |
| Social grade | AB; C1; C2; DE (shares of the graded population) |
| National identity | all 8 categories |

* SDZ counts are shared out across that SDZ's DZs in proportion to their
  "Other Christian" count. "Remaining Other Christian" is what's left, so no
  religion feature is nested inside another.
* Lapsed shares are left unclipped. They dip just below zero in a few DZs,
  where the two tables' disclosure noise disagrees.
* Age bands were tested and dropped.

**Model:**

* **Joint multinomial logit** across the nine parties.
  * **Symmetric**: every party has coefficients, and every category of every
    group is included. There is no reference party or category.
  * DZ shares always cover all nine parties: every party's voters, whether or
    not it stood.
* **Mapped onto each DEA's ballot through the transfer matrix.** A party that
  stood keeps its voters. An absent party's voters go to the parties that stood,
  in proportion to its transfer-matrix row renormalised over them. That is a
  **direct renormalisation**, not a cascade through other absent parties,
  matching how the matrix was estimated (see caveats).
* **Fitted at DZ level against DEA results.** Each DZ is predicted from its own
  features, summed to its DEA weighted by electorate (turnout flat within a
  DEA), put through the DEA's ballot, and scored against the DEA's council
  result (vote-weighted log-likelihood). Fitting at the level the model is applied at removes the
  aggregation bias that flattens a DEA-fitted model.
* **Features standardised.** At prediction time they are clipped to the
  training range extended by **0.5 of that range each side**. This guard was
  decided when the prior was fitted on DEAs and applied to the more extreme
  DZs: against the DEA range it clipped about 22% of DZs. Since the prior trains
  on DZs, the range already covers every DZ. The final fit clips none, and
  cross-validation clips 1 of 3,780 held-out DZs. **It is now effectively
  inactive**, and only matters if the model is applied to data outside
  training.
* **Bloc-structured ridge penalty.**
  * Within-bloc penalty: each party's deviation from its bloc's mean
    coefficients.
  * Bloc penalty: the size of the bloc means.
  * Blocs: nationalist = SF, SDLP, PBP, Aontú; unionist = DUP, UUP, TUV;
    other = Alliance, Green. PBP and Aontú are placed with the nationalists for
    now, knowing the fit isn't perfect.
  * Intercepts unpenalised.
* **Penalties by cross-validation minimum** (leave one council out, 11 folds):
  **bloc 0.1, within-bloc 3.16**, an interior minimum.
  * Grid: bloc 0.001–1, within-bloc 0.1–10. Every fit starts from zero, and a
    check at 31.6 and 100 was worse.
  * The curve near the minimum:

    | within-bloc | 1 | 3.16 | 10 | 31.6 |
    |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
    | KL | 53.5 | **52.2** | 52.9 | 54.5 |

  * Earlier curves reused each fit as the next one's starting point. Under v2
    that left some fits short of their optimum and hid this minimum. Full-data
    fits reach the same solution from any start.
* **Slope correction tau = 1.1.** All slopes are scaled by 1.1, then the
  intercepts are refitted. This trades about 0.6 mnats of DEA-level fit for
  realistic segregation extremes, which DEA-level validation cannot see.
  * The transfer mapping alone removed much of v1's over-mixing, so tau came
    down from v1's 1.2.

    | tau | 1.0 | 1.1 | 1.2 |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | KL | 52.7 | 53.3 | 55.0 |
    | DZ extremes | 3.0% / 3.7% | **2.2% / 2.7%** | 1.6% / 2.0% |

    (DZ extremes are defined in [Evidence](#evidence).)
* **Output shares are unmasked** (all nine parties present). The prior
  describes voters, not a ballot paper.

## Prior covariance

How the prior's errors vary and covary between parties. It is used to **weight
the prior against election evidence** in the calibration fit, not as a final
confidence interval. All steps are in `scripts/prior_covariance.py`.

1. **Out-of-sample errors.** Refit the prior with each council held out, and
   predict its DEAs through each DEA's ballot.
2. **Log-ratio representation.** Errors are proportional, so small parties are
   not pushed toward zero too cheaply. Shares are floored at 0.2%.
   *Implementation choice:* the floor value, DEAs unweighted.
3. **Variation matrix.**
   * For each party pair, take the variance of (predicted − actual)
     log(p/q), over DEAs where both stood.
   * Convert to a clr covariance: S = −½ HTH.
   * Pairwise log-ratios need no common set of parties. The first attempt
     centred over the parties that stood in each DEA, which produced an invalid
     matrix (min eigenvalue −0.88).
4. **One global matrix** for all areas; area-varying uncertainty is a
   follow-up.
5. **Shrinkage toward bloc-structured targets.**
   * Each correlation is pulled toward the overlap-weighted mean of its pair
     type, by λ = k/(k+n), where n = DEAs where both stood.
   * **Six pair types**: within each bloc, and three separate cross-bloc
     types, since e.g. SDLP–Green shouldn't be forced to resemble SF–TUV.
   * **k = 10 for nationalist–other pairs, 60 for all others.** These are
     rounded from a council-block bootstrap estimate on prior v3 (noise
     c = 1.96):
     * nationalist–other k ≈ 11;
     * unionist–other ≈ 77;
     * pooled over all types ≈ 59;
     * within-bloc and nationalist–unionist: no spread beyond noise.

     v1's estimates had given 20 and 200.
   * *Implementation choice:* the bootstrap as the noise estimate.
6. **Repair.** One eigenvalue came out at −0.006 and was **clipped to zero**.
   The largest change to any correlation is 0.013, against 0.0015 for v1.
7. **Scaling to larger regions (Method B).** Keep the DEA-scale correlations
   and scale the variances by (region population / DEA population)^β, with
   **common β = −0.35**. This is recorded in the output and applied downstream.
   At constituency size (~4.6× a DEA) the variance factor is 0.59.
   * Measured on prior v3: class means −0.35, per party −0.25 (Green) to
     −0.48 (UUP). v1's measurement gave −0.37, which was then set to −0.4.
   * Method A was rejected: it would build the parliamentary layers'
     uncertainty from council results that are then combined with those same
     layers.

Resulting DEA-scale values:

| | SF | DUP | Alliance | UUP | SDLP | TUV | Green | PBP | Aontú |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| error SD (log-ratio) | 0.30 | 0.32 | 0.40 | 0.43 | 0.63 | 0.38 | 0.61 | 0.63 | 0.42 |

| Pair type | within nationalist | within unionist | within other | nationalist–unionist | nationalist–other | unionist–other |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| target correlation | −0.20 | −0.20 | −0.38 | +0.04 | −0.12 | +0.07 |

A 9-party clr matrix averages −0.125 by construction. Within-bloc pairs now
show a modest trade-off beyond that baseline (−0.20). Nationalist–other stays
negative and unionist–other positive.

## Transfer matrix v0

Where a party's voters go when that party isn't standing. Calibration needs it
in reverse: in a DEA without TUV, some TUV voters are already inside the DUP
and UUP totals. Simulation needs it forwards. v0 was built with a
minimum-viable-product mindset: data-based, but simple.

**Data** (`scripts/parse_transfers.py`):

* Stage-by-stage transfers from all 18 Assembly workbooks and 73 of 80 council
  DEAs. That is every council xlsx and text PDF; the 7 scanned Fermanagh &
  Omagh sheets are excluded.
* An **event** is one stage with a single source candidate: an elimination, or
  the transfer of a surplus.
* Skipped:
  * stages where several candidates were excluded together (46), since their
    flows can't be split;
  * stages that don't balance (15). These are mostly undistributed final
    eliminations; Belfast East's stage 11 is an error on the sheet itself.
* Three council PDFs (Crotlieve, Downpatrick, Slieve Gullion) never print their
  final stages.
* Parsing checks:
  * PDF columns are accepted only if every printed running total chains.
  * Surplus vs elimination is decided by whether the source had reached quota.
    This agrees with the sheet's own label at all 332 labelled stages.
* Parties, including the independents counted as parties, come from the
  first-preference JSONs. Independents and micro-parties are transparent:
  transfers from them are ignored, and transfers to them are dropped with the
  rest renormalised.

**Clean events** (`scripts/fit_transfer_matrix.py`):

* **No running mate still in the count.** Otherwise same-party transfers swamp
  the cross-party signal.
* **At most 25% of what's passed on came from other parties' voters.**
  First preferences and transfers from a running mate count as the party's own.
  For a surplus, only the last parcel received is examined, as in NI counts,
  so the test applies to that parcel.
* Result: 221 clean events from 506 single-source events.

  | From | SF | TUV | UUP | Alliance | SDLP | DUP | Green | Aontú | PBP |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | clean events | 48 | 35 | 32 | 28 | 24 | 18 | 18 | 11 | 7 |

**Model:**

* **Non-transferable votes are renormalised away**, consistent with flat
  turnout. Aontú's transfers are 36% non-transferable, the highest.
* **Availability-adjusted.**
  * Each source party has one weight per destination party.
  * In an event, a destination's share is its weight divided by the total
    weight of the parties still in the count. Weights are fitted by the standard
    minorise-maximise update.
  * The matrix is applied the same way: renormalise a row over the parties
    standing. So it is still proportional *within* whoever stands, but with
    party-specific rates rather than first-preference sizes.
  * Pooled percentages were visibly wrong without this. For example,
    Alliance→Green was 4% because Green was rarely still in the count.
* **Every event counts once**, whatever its size. Weighting by votes gave
  similar results.
* **Shrinkage toward bloc expectations.**
  * Each row gets **k = 5** pseudo-events in which all eight parties are
    available, split by target shares. Pairs seen in few events end up near the
    target; well-observed pairs follow their data.
  * Targets: the same fit pooled over every source party in the bloc, with the
    party itself left out and the rest renormalised. For example, SDLP's target
    uses the pooled SF, SDLP, PBP and Aontú events.
  * **No all-party fallback.** Targets are bloc expectations, so DUP voters'
    behaviour must not inform Alliance→Green.
  * **Alliance→Green and Green→Alliance targets are hand-set at 35%**, as a
    placeholder. The "other" bloc has no bloc-mates to pool.

**Result**: % of a party's transferring voters going to each other party if
all eight stood (rows sum to 100).

| From \ to | SF | DUP | All | UUP | SDLP | TUV | Grn | PBP | Aon |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **SF** | — | 0 | 16 | 1 | 53 | 0 | 9 | 9 | 12 |
| **DUP** | 0 | — | 4 | 55 | 2 | 35 | 3 | 1 | 0 |
| **Alliance** | 7 | 2 | — | 11 | 28 | 1 | 32 | 15 | 4 |
| **UUP** | 0 | 44 | 18 | — | 5 | 24 | 6 | 2 | 1 |
| **SDLP** | 19 | 1 | 31 | 3 | — | 1 | 19 | 11 | 16 |
| **TUV** | 0 | 62 | 3 | 29 | 2 | — | 3 | 1 | 0 |
| **Green** | 10 | 2 | 35 | 5 | 20 | 1 | — | 25 | 4 |
| **PBP** | 11 | 1 | 20 | 2 | 32 | 1 | 22 | — | 11 |
| **Aontú** | 27 | 4 | 9 | 1 | 35 | 2 | 11 | 11 | — |

* **Driven entirely by the target:** SDLP→Aontú and PBP→Aontú have no events
  at all. SDLP→Aontú at 16% comes from SF-dominated nationalist pooling and may
  be high.
* **Most affected by shrinkage:** PBP (7 events) and Aontú (11). Unshrunk,
  PBP→Green is 38% (22% shrunk) and Aontú→SF 37% (27%).
* **Sensitivity to k:** from k = 2 to 10, thin pairs move by up to 8 points,
  and well-observed pairs by 1–3.
* **Pooling side-effect:** each unionist row is pulled slightly toward the
  bloc's combined pattern, e.g. UUP→DUP 40% unshrunk, 44% shrunk.

## Turnout v0

How many ballots a Data Zone casts, as against how many electors live in it.
Everything else in the model is a *share*; this is the weight those shares
carry.

What is fitted is an **index**, not a turnout, because the level belongs to the
election and the place only says how far above or below it sits:

    votes in a zone = electorate x level(election) x index(zone)

| Election | Level | Regions | Range recorded |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Council 2023 | 53.7% | 80 DEAs | 41.4–69.9% |
| Assembly 2022 | 62.9% | 18 | 57.7–68.8% |
| Westminster 2024 | 57.2% | 18 | 52.2–65.6% |

Every one of those 116 regions carries a measured electorate and valid-vote
count in its own result sheet, so the target needs no estimating. All three are
fitted at once: each contributes its own level, and they share the
coefficients.

**Features** are three, chosen by leave-one-region-out CV over nested candidate
sets rather than by eye — 116 regions cannot tell 24 collinear census shares
apart:

| Candidate set | CV rms error in log turnout |
|---|---:|
| grade_DE | 108.0 |
| + grade_AB | 104.1 |
| + age 65 and over | 101.4 |
| **grade_DE, grade_AB, up_catholic** | **78.2** |
| + age 65 and over | 79.4 |
| + id_other | 80.8 |

Age earns nothing once the other three are in and is dropped. The fitted
coefficients are `grade_DE −0.76`, `grade_AB −0.26`, `up_catholic +0.26`: the
most deprived places vote least, the most affluent slightly less than the
middle, and — at equal social grade — a Catholic-background area votes more.
That last is the largest single gain in the table (104 → 78) and it is the one
with consequences, because it moves the balance between blocs in any region
drawn. Within-region correlation against what was recorded is +0.76 at council,
+0.78 at Assembly, +0.51 at Westminster.

**The level follows region size under STV** (see [In the app](#in-the-app)):
the same two anchors, log-linear between them and clamped outside, because 80
small regions is a council election and 18 large ones is an Assembly election
and nothing else in a drawn map says which is meant. First past the post has
only Westminster to go on and keeps its single level.

The index is held inside the range a real region has actually managed
(0.77–1.30, the most and least eager region with its election's level divided
out), which binds on 5.1% of zones; before clipping it reaches 0.66.

**Turnout is not a response to the contest.** This is what makes it safe to
attach to a place: across the 18 Westminster seats, turnout against the winning
margin correlates **+0.18** — near zero, and if anything the wrong way round,
with the tightest seats in the country (East Londonderry, North Antrim) among
the quietest. Were it otherwise, fitting turnout per zone would bake today's
boundaries into the zones themselves and every drawn map would inherit them.

### What it is worth, measured

Honestly: **less than it looks**, and the measurement is worth keeping because
the intuition is so strongly the other way.

Aggregating the fitted shares to the real regions and mapping them onto the
ballot actually offered, the mean absolute error per party per region is:

| Weighting | Council | Assembly | Westminster |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| By electors | 2.16% | 1.35% | 3.11% |
| By votes (modelled turnout) | 2.16% | 1.40% | 3.22% |
| By votes (**measured** turnout) | — | 1.35% | 3.13% |

The third row is the ceiling a perfect turnout model could reach, using each
DEA's actual recorded poll, and it is no better than ignoring turnout
altogether. So the weighting question is immaterial to *accuracy*: what remains
at 1.35% and 3.11% is cross-election drift, the floors and the prior, all of
them an order of magnitude larger than anything turnout moves. (The modelled
index is also only +0.41 correlated with measured turnout at zone level, against
+0.76 at the region level it was fitted on, so some of its own noise is in
there.)

What the refit to vote weights *did* buy, end to end, is small but real and in
the right direction throughout:

| | v2 / v0 (electors) | v3 / v1 (votes) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Prior, in-sample KL per vote | 33.5 | 33.3 |
| Share error, council | 2.18% | 2.16% |
| Share error, Assembly | 1.39% | 1.35% |
| Share error, Westminster | 3.16% | 3.11% |

And what turnout buys in the app is not accuracy at all but **arithmetic that
was previously wrong**: the number of ballots a region casts, the size of an STV
quota, and the standing thresholds, which are absolute vote counts and so were
misapplied wherever turnout was not average. It also makes a point the tool
exists to make — an equal-population region is not an equal-voter region.

## Fitting (voters v1)

Combines prior v3 with the three elections into one estimate of voter shares
per DZ (`scripts/fit_voters.py`). The layers are the 2023 council (80 DEAs),
the 2022 Assembly (18 constituencies, 2008 boundaries) and 2024 Westminster
(18 constituencies, 2024 boundaries). Each DZ belongs to one region per layer;
DZs split by a constituency boundary go to the constituency holding most of
them.

**1. One shift per region.**
* In each layer, each region gets one log-ratio shift `a_r`, applied to all of
  its DZs: `x_z ∝ p_z · exp(a_r)`. The region's result sets the overall level,
  and demographics still spread it across the region's DZs.
* **Prior term:** `a_r ~ N(0, Σ_r)`, where Σ_r is the prior covariance scaled by
  (region population / average DEA population)^−0.35.
* **Prediction:** the electorate-weighted sum of the region's DZ shares, put
  through the region's ballot with the transfer matrix.
* **Absent parties** need no special case. The ballot mapping shows where their
  voters went, and one direction per absent party (that party against its own
  transfer mix) is left to the prior and to other layers.
* Rejected: separate adjustments per DZ, which would need correlations of
  prior errors between DZs that we don't have.

**2. Data term: soft, in log-ratios.**
* The observed log-ratios among parties that stood are Gaussian around the
  prediction, with variance = a per-layer floor + counting noise. A plain
  multinomial was rejected: thousands of votes would force an exact fit.
* The floor stands for everything a result doesn't share with true voter
  preferences: election type, candidates, local campaigns, swing between
  elections. It is the same for every party pair, and scaled to region size
  like the prior.
* Floors, as pairwise log-ratio SDs at constituency scale: **council 0.3,
  Assembly 0.2, Westminster 0.55**. The council floor is about 0.39 at a
  typical DEA.
* The floors were measured from how much real results disagree across layers.
  Each layer's votes were spread to DZs by the prior's pattern, summed onto
  the other layer's constituencies, and compared for party pairs standing in
  both. Counting noise was removed.

  | Layer pair | Log-ratio RMS disagreement |
  |---|---:|
  | council vs Assembly | 0.34 |
  | council vs Westminster | 0.61 |
  | Assembly vs Westminster | 0.57 |

  Solving the three equations gives floors of 0.29 / 0.18 / 0.54. Systematic
  offsets between election types are **included**: the election-type factor is
  deferred, so they are unexplained error for now.
* Westminster's large floor is plausible given FPTP tactical voting,
  stand-asides and fewer candidates.

**3. Each layer's uncertainty from curvature.**
`C_r = (JᵀΩ⁻¹J + Σ_r⁻¹)⁻¹` at the fitted shift, where J is how the predicted
log-ratios respond to the shift. This is the principled version of perturbing
pairs of parties. A layer is confident in the directions its result observes
and falls back to the prior's uncertainty in the directions it can't see.

**4. Combining the layers.**
* Each layer's **data contribution** `D = JᵀΩ⁻¹J` is combined, not its whole
  fit. Otherwise the prior would be counted once per layer and pull the
  estimate back toward itself.
* **Within-region spread.** A region's shift applies to a given DZ only
  approximately, because local areas differ from their region in ways
  demographics miss. That is where the earlier 1/sqrt(pop) weighting went: a
  variance term, not a multiplier.
  * `H_r = h² · (region population / constituency population)^0.5`, with
    **h = 0.6**, the same for every party pair.
  * It inflates each data contribution in the directions it observes:
    `D̃ = (D⁻¹ + H_r)⁻¹`.
  * h was measured as the spread of council DEAs' out-of-sample residuals
    around their constituency's residual: 0.59 (2008 boundaries) / 0.63 (2024
    boundaries), after removing the council floor's local part. Both are
    probably overestimates.
* **Per DZ,** on top of its DEA's prior:
  `C_z⁻¹ = Σ_DEA⁻¹ + Σ_L D̃_L`, `m_z = C_z Σ_L D̃_L u_L`, and shares
  `x_z ∝ p_z exp(m_z)`.
  * At a typical DZ, the observed-direction weights come out roughly council 1
    : Assembly 0.8 : Westminster 0.5.
* Everything is computed in the 8 free log-ratio dimensions.

### Weighting and scaling

* **Layer weight exponent.**
  * Test: council DEAs were merged into contiguous regions, the regions'
    results redistributed by demographics, and the misallocation measured
    against region size.
  * Allocation error variance grows as about pop^0.6. Neither 0.5 nor 1.0 is
    excluded.
  * **1/sqrt(pop)** was chosen as the principled round form.
* **β for prior error.**
  * Regions: single DEAs, adjacent same-council pairs, councils.
  * Metric: pairwise log-ratio error of region totals.
  * Result: class means −0.37, per party −0.32 (Green) to −0.48 (Alliance,
    UUP). A common value is well supported.
  * For comparison, fully independent errors would give −1: errors are
    strongly spatially clustered.

### Campaign concentration

How much does a concentrated campaign move one seat? Measured by comparing each
party's share in a constituency with what the same ground gave at the other
elections, spreading those results to Data Zones by the model's own pattern.
Moves are in centred log shares, so only relative movement counts.

The ceiling is about **×2.2 of a party's share**: Belfast South & Mid Down SDLP
20.7% → 49.1% (×2.37), Fermanagh & South Tyrone UUP ×2.03, South Antrim UUP
×2.06. Squeezes are as large the other way — Belfast East UUP ×0.28, Lagan
Valley SDLP ×0.31.

Spread of the effect by party (log share, Westminster against the Assembly's
ground, 121 party-seat cases):

| | SF | PBP | DUP | Alliance | TUV | Aontú | Green | SDLP | UUP |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| spread | 0.11 | 0.19 | 0.24 | 0.27 | 0.27 | 0.28 | 0.40 | 0.48 | 0.53 |

* **Mid-sized parties move most.** Not the smallest: PBP and Aontú are steadier
  than UUP and SDLP, so this is not a small-numbers artefact.
* **It is mostly a first-past-the-post phenomenon.** The same comparison between
  two STV elections — Assembly against council — gives roughly half the spread
  (SF 0.10, UUP 0.27) and a largest move of ×1.8.
* **It is what the Westminster floor stands for.** That floor is 0.55 pairwise,
  about 0.39 a party; UUP, SDLP and Green sit at or above it, SF far below.

**Is it incentive or capacity?** Each case was scored by how close the party was
to leading on the other elections' ground, and the move compared:

| Position | n | Mean move |
|---|---:|---:|
| Safe lead | 6 | ×1.00 |
| Narrow lead | 12 | ×1.20 |
| Close behind | 21 | ×1.19 |
| Middling | 34 | ×0.94 |
| Far behind | 48 | ×0.92 |

twelfth — but that accounts for only **9% of the variance**, and the per-party
spreads are unchanged by controlling for it (UUP 0.53 → 0.54, SDLP 0.48 → 0.44,
SF 0.11 → 0.09). So the asymmetry is party-shaped, not position-shaped.

## Assumptions and caveats

* **Intention is fixed across election types.** This is known to be faulty: for
  example, SF and Green run higher at council, and SDLP, UUP and Alliance higher
  at Westminster, though standing confounds all of it.
* **Turnout** varies by election type and now by place too
  ([turnout v0](#turnout-v0)); it is still flat across *party*, so within one
  zone every party's voters are equally likely to turn out. Layers combine as
  **shares**, not votes.
* **Absent parties' voters go directly to the parties standing.**
  * Each absent party's transfer-matrix row is renormalised over the parties
    standing. A B voter who would have picked another absent party C is
    treated as a typical B voter, not a C voter.
  * A cascade (B→C→…) would make the opposite assumption. The truth is probably
    in between. **To consider going forward.**
  * Within whoever stands, shares still follow fixed party-specific rates (the
    matrix's proportional form).
* **Selection.** Parties stand where they are strong. A party's prior in areas
  where it never stood is extrapolated from where it did.
* **Religion and identity shares include children.** No religion-by-age
  breakdown exists per DZ.
* **Collinearity.** For example, up_protestant ~ id_british 0.96. Individual
  coefficients are not interpretable, only predictions.
* **Sparse features at DZ level** get amplified by standardisation. Free
  Presbyterian is zero in 27% of DZs.
* **DZ extremes can't be validated.** No results below DEA level are
  published; tau was chosen by judgement against the most extreme real DEAs.
* **Global covariance over-trusts the prior in heartlands.** Example: TUV's
  out-of-sample SD was 1.8 points in 30–55% unionist DEAs vs 7.0 points above
  55%.
* **Local micro-party caveats:** PUP 13.7% in Coleraine, CCLA 6.6% in
  Enniskillen.
* **Electorate within a DEA** follows adult population, so a DEA's registration
  ratio applies uniformly to its DZs.
* **STV transfers are not Westminster votes.** Lower preferences cost a voter
  nothing, so they probably overstate cross-party willingness compared with an
  actual FPTP vote. Non-transferable votes (a proxy for abstaining) are not
  modelled.
* **Transfer events are selective.** Parties are observed transferring mainly
  when eliminated or when a single candidate is elected early, and
  multi-candidate exclusions (often small parties together) are lost.
* **The transfer matrix pools elections and areas.** One matrix covers the
  whole of NI, the Assembly and council together.
* **Council results are used twice.** The prior was fitted to them, and the
  council layer then fits them again. The prior's covariance comes from
  out-of-sample errors, so it overstates the prior's uncertainty at those DEAs,
  which lets council data dominate slightly more.
* **Floors and h are rough.**
  * Each comparison covers only 18 constituencies, and party pairs within a
    constituency aren't independent.
  * The floors mix election-type effects, swing (1–2 years apart) and
    allocation error.
  * h probably overstates the within-region spread.
* **Linearised uncertainty.** Curvature-based uncertainty and combining data
  contributions are exact only for a linear fit. The ballot mapping and
  log-ratios make it mildly non-linear.
* **One shift per region.** Within a DEA, DZs differ only by demographics. The
  spread term widens the uncertainty but doesn't change the point estimate.

## In the app

The map draws seats from `voters_v1_dz.csv`, under first past the post or STV: `scripts/build_app_voters.py` writes each DZ's electorate,
voter shares and [turnout index](#turnout-v0) to `web/data/dz_voters.json`, and
a zone's votes are

    electorate x turnout index x the level of the election being simulated

First past the post has one measured level and keeps it: 57.2%, Westminster
2024. **Under STV the level follows how big the regions are**, because a council
election and an Assembly election are both STV and poll 53.7% against 62.9% —
and the size of a region is the only thing a drawn map can offer to tell them
apart. It is log-linear between the two measured anchors (DEA-sized regions of
about 17,300 electors, constituency-sized ones of about 76,300) and never
outside them, so 18 regions polls at the Assembly's level, 80 at a council's,
and 50 somewhere between:

| Regions | Electors each | Level |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 18 or fewer | 76,700+ | 62.9% |
| 30 | 46,000 | 59.6% |
| 50 | 27,600 | 56.5% |
| 80 or more | 17,300 or fewer | 53.7% |

This is a **proxy, not a cause**: what really separates the two elections is how
much they matter to people, and region size is merely what correlates with it
and is visible to the app. Nothing between the anchors is evidence.

The level scales a whole region alike and so changes no winner; the index does
not, and is the reason an equal-population region is not an equal-voter one. See
"Variables" in [the README](../README.md#variables).

### Tactical voting

Under first past the post, voters whose party cannot win often back someone who
can. Northern Ireland rarely offers a tidy two-horse race — three parties are
often live, and sometimes the real contest is for second — so nothing here
ranks the parties. Each has a **viability**, and everything follows from it:

| | |
|---|---|
| viability | how close a party is to the leader, falling smoothly with the gap; a party 8 points behind is half live |
| doubt | how spread that viability is: one dominant party means none, two or three in contention means plenty |
| desertion | `0.28 × doubt × (1 − viability)` — you leave when your own party cannot win *and* the seat is in doubt, so a safe seat squeezes nobody however hopeless they are |
| destination | every other party, in proportion to its viability times this party's preference for it from the transfer matrix |

Votes move, none are lost, and nothing happens under STV, where a lower
preference costs a voter nothing. It can be switched off in the app.

**Calibration** is anchored on the measured squeeze — parties out of contention
keep about six-sevenths of their vote in the tightest seats and nearly all of it
in safe ones ([Campaign concentration](#campaign-concentration)). The model
reproduces that without tuning:

| Top-two gap | Measured | Model |
|---|---:|---:|
| Tightest | ×0.86 | ×0.88 |
| Close | ×0.91 | ×0.92 |
| Safe | ×0.96 | ×0.98 |

**What is reasoned rather than measured:** the shape. Viability, doubt and the
destination rule are argued from how tactical voting works, not fitted — our
data cannot distinguish them from a cruder rank-based rule, because candidate
effects of up to ×2.4 swamp the comparison. One pass is taken rather than
chasing the fixed point where the switching changes the viabilities that caused
it.

**How much it does:** across 8 drawn maps and 144 seats it changed the winner in
3, all in seats decided by under 5 points — about a fifth of seats are that
close. On the real 2024 boundaries it changes no winners. It is a few points in
a marginal, not a mechanism that rescues the seats the model gets wrong.

### Who stands

The model estimates a party's *voters*; whether it is on the ballot is applied
afterwards, in the region model. Two things can happen to a party, both of them
one mapping from true voters to ballot entities, applied once to the per-zone
votes, and both driven from the app's party editor:

* **Standing aside.** Its voters abstain at that party's exhaustion rate — the
  share with no further preference — and the rest go to the parties that do
  stand, by its transfer row renormalised over them. Directly, not cascading
  through other absent parties, as in the prior.
* **Merging.** Its votes move to the host party's slot in full. A merger is
  taken to keep every voter; that is an assumption, not a measurement, and the
  defections a real merger would cause are not modelled.

Both are global — a party is in the world or out of it — and the standing
thresholds below then decide, region by region, where a party that is in the
world actually fields a candidate.

A merged party's own behaviour in a count is derived from its members: its
transfer row is theirs averaged by votes with the now-internal flows removed
and the rest renormalised, its column is theirs summed, and its exhaustion rate
is theirs averaged. Note what the first of those means in practice — for a
DUP + UUP + TUV merger the large flows between the three vanish, so where the
merged party's voters go next rests on the small non-unionist tail of each
party's row (Alliance 49%, Green 23%, SDLP 16%).

### Standing thresholds (standing v0)

No party contests every region, so each has a **threshold**: the support it
needs locally before it puts up a candidate. Below it the party is off that
region's ballot, its voters abstain at its exhaustion rate and the rest
transfer, exactly as for standing aside above. On by default, and switchable
off in the app.

The two elections turn out to be asking different questions, and are fitted
separately (`scripts/fit_standing.py`, `data/model/standing_v0.json`):

| | SF | DUP | All | UUP | SDLP | TUV | Grn | PBP | Aontú |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **STV**, voters | 328 | 318 | always | 160 | 171 | 476 | 104 | 274 | 289 |
| accuracy | 96% | 99% | 98% | 97% | 94% | 82% | 85% | 85% | 86% |
| **FPTP**, share | 9.5% | always | always | always | always | 2.1% | 0.9% | 3.3% | 1.0% |
| accuracy | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | 83% | 94% | 94% | 83% |

The vote counts are ballots, so they move with turnout: they were refitted when
[turnout v0](#turnout-v0) arrived, each election's regions measured at that
election's own level rather than all three at a flat 57.2%.

**Why the units differ.** Under STV standing is a question of *capacity* — can
you find a candidate and a branch to run them — so the bar is an absolute
number of voters. That is not a stylistic choice: council DEAs and Assembly
constituencies differ about four and a half fold in size, and pooling the two
(80 + 18 regions), a threshold in votes beats one in share for every small
party — TUV 84% against 77%, Green 86% / 76%, PBP 86% / 81%, Aontú 86% / 83% —
while the large parties are indifferent. It also explains away the puzzle that
the Assembly looks like "everyone always stands": a constituency is simply big
enough that the small parties clear the same bar they miss in half the DEAs.
Because it is absolute, it carries to drawn regions of any size — one giant
region has everybody standing, which is what a national list would look like.

Under first past the post standing is a question of *viability* instead: the
thresholds are highest for the **biggest** parties, because what they are
deciding is whether a seat is winnable at all. Every Westminster seat is much
the same size, so that one is fitted on share.

**Pacts are excluded.** Three of the 2024 absences were stand-asides, not
judgements of strength: the DUP in Fermanagh and South Tyrone (17.3% of the
modelled vote there), the UUP in Belfast North (7.2%), and Sinn Féin in Belfast
South and Mid Down (19.0%), each leaving a clear run to a neighbour. A pact is a
deal rather than a rule about support, and left in it would have handed the DUP
and UUP fitted thresholds of 9% and 7% on the strength of one seat apiece. With
them out, Sinn Féin's remaining absences are genuinely hopeless seats (2.7%,
5.2%, 8.8%) and the rule separates them perfectly. Pacts themselves stay
[deferred](#deferred-past-v0).

**A merged party's threshold** is its members' mean, weighted by their NI-wide
votes, so a small party joining a big one inherits most of the big one's reach.

**How much it does.** Run on the real boundaries, the rule leaves about as many
ballots short as really were, and the seats barely move — which is the point: it
is there to make the ballot look right, not to change who wins.

| Loaded boundaries | Empty slots, model | Actually empty | Seats moved |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Council 2023, 80 × 5 | 255 of 720 | 246 | none of 400 |
| Assembly 2022, 18 × 5 | 7 of 162 | 12 | none of 90 |
| Westminster 2024, 18 × 1 | 34 of 162 | 40 | none of 18 |

The council figure is the one that moves with turnout, and it is a good check on
the level following region size: with one flat STV level it read 222 against the
246 really empty, because a council-sized region was handed an Assembly-sized
poll and its parties cleared an absolute threshold too easily. Polled at its own
level it reads 255. The Assembly and Westminster rows under-count a little
throughout, which is the small parties' rules being noisier than a single
threshold can express.

The three layers differ by a factor of twenty-five in how often a party sits a
region out, and one set of thresholds gets all three about right — the strongest
evidence that the absolute-number form is the right one for STV.

**What is decided rather than measured:**

* A **hard** threshold, not a probability. Either way the optimiser can see the
  edge and game it; a hard rule at least says plainly what it is doing, and a
  die re-rolled between runs would make two scores incomparable for reasons
  that have nothing to do with the map.
* Where no party clears its bar — a tiny region — the largest stands anyway, so
  every region holds an election.
* The score's average and extreme modes still measure the underlying voters,
  which is the demographic quantity they exist to describe; gerrymander mode
  scores the election as actually held.

**Circularity worth remembering:** the shares are voters v1, which was itself
fitted with who-stood-where applied. The accuracies above describe the pattern;
they are not an out-of-sample test of it. The small parties' rules are also
noisy in a way the numbers hide — TUV stood at 4.6% of Bangor Central but not
at 5.6% of Bangor East, which is organisation, not strength.

### The STV count

Simulating a real count would need each party's candidates and every voter's
ranking, neither of which we have. Instead every party is **one entity holding
all its votes**:

* **Quota** = votes / (seats + 1), fixed at the start as in a real count.
* **Election:** a party at a quota takes a seat and keeps the remainder, which
  is what a running mate would inherit.
* **Exclusion:** with nobody at a quota, the weakest party goes out. Its pile
  loses that party's **exhaustion** share — the voters with no further
  preference — and the rest moves on by [transfer matrix
  v0](#transfer-matrix-v0), renormalised over the parties still in.
* **Endgame:** one party left takes the seats that remain.

Exhaustion rates come from the same clean transfer events the matrix was
fitted on: SF 12%, UUP 11%, Green 10%, PBP 13%, DUP and SDLP 15%, Alliance
17%, TUV 19%, Aontú 36%.

**Why this method.** Four rules were measured against 91 real STV contests —
the 2022 Assembly and the 73 council DEAs whose winners are readable — fed the
real first preferences, so the comparison is of the seat rule alone:

| Method | Seats wrong of 499 | Regions exactly right |
|---|---:|---:|
| Largest remainder (quota, then remainders) | 30 | 61/91 |
| Bloc-pooled quota | 48 | 44/91 |
| Party-level STV | 25 | 66/91 |
| **Party-level STV + exhaustion** | **25** | **66/91** |

The accuracy gap is modest; the bias is the reason to prefer the count. Largest
remainder never lets a transfer happen, so it is systematically wrong — SF +12
seats, SDLP −9. The party-level count's errors are scattered: within ±5 seats
for every party, and ±3 once exhaustion is included. A systematic bias would
show up as a fake advantage every time the lines move, which is exactly what
this tool is for.

What no party-level count can see is what the remaining 25 seats are mostly
about: vote management, running mates and individual candidates.

**Steering it.** In gerrymander mode the score for a region is

  `seat bonus × seats won + leftover votes ÷ quota`

both taken from the count. The staircase dominates, so a seat always beats
vote-building, while the leftover pile gives the optimiser a gradient pointing
at the next seat — including the transfers the party would pick up on the way,
since that pile is the count's own. The seat bonus defaults to 2. Average and
extreme modes still work on the party's share.

## Deferred past v0

* **Pacts.** Per-region standing is now built as a threshold on support
  ([standing v0](#standing-thresholds-standing-v0)), which covers a party
  skipping a region it is weak in. What it cannot express is a party skipping a
  region it is *strong* in to give an ally a clear run — the DUP in Fermanagh
  and South Tyrone, Sinn Féin in Belfast South. Measured and **not recommended**
  for building; the findings, since the question will come round again:

  * **The arithmetic does not predict the real pacts.** Form a pact wherever a
    bloc's split costs it the seat, and on the real boundaries the rule fires in
    4 of 18 seats (East Londonderry, Upper Bann, North Down, Belfast East) —
    none of them a seat where a pact actually happened. The reason is that no
    2024 pact was decisive: the DUP stood aside in Fermanagh and South Tyrone
    and Sinn Féin still won by 8.9%; the UUP stood aside in Belfast North, SF by
    13.9%; Sinn Féin stood aside in Belfast South and the SDLP won by 28.8%,
    which it would have anyway. Meanwhile East Londonderry — decided by 0.4%,
    the tightest seat in Northern Ireland, and one a unionist pact would plainly
    have taken — had no pact at all. Poor recall and poor precision on the only
    cases we can see. Pacts are bloc solidarity and habit (the same handful of
    seats recur since 2015) as much as calculation.
  * **Tactical voting already does most of the work.** With the viability model
    on, the seats a pact would flip on real boundaries drop from 4 to 1 of 18.
    Voters in a tight seat are already deserting the weaker bloc partner, so a
    formal pact adds about a third of what its raw transfer implies — and
    modelling both risks counting the same coordination twice.
  * **On a gerrymandered map it depends entirely on how disciplined the pact
    is.** Over 3 gerrymander targets × 2 seeds (108 seats), letting a bloc fold
    *every* junior partner at once moved 10 seats — Sinn Féin's 14-seat map
    falls to 10 on both seeds. Restricted to the pairwise pacts that actually
    occur, one partner stepping back, it moved 2. A gerrymander against
    unionism works by splitting it three ways, and folding either the UUP or
    TUV alone does not clear Sinn Féin; only folding both does. **Gerrymandering
    a three-way split bloc is robust to pairwise coordination and vulnerable
    only to total coordination.**
  * **Pacts are a unionist instrument here.** A DUP-drawn map offered no
    available pact at all on either seed: its victim is the nationalist bloc,
    which is not split — SF at 30.3% against the SDLP's 9.3% — so there is
    nothing to coordinate. Unionism is the fragmented bloc (23.3 / 11.0 / 6.1),
    so it is the one with a counter-move.
  * **The party editor already covers the version that matters.** "What if
    unionism ran one candidate?" is a DUP + UUP + TUV merge, available now, and
    by the measurement above that global merge is exactly the disciplined case
    with real consequences. A pact feature would add the per-region refinement
    of something the app already does bluntly, for the case worth 2 seats in
    108.
  * **It could not be validated in any case.** The three real pact seats are
    among the ones the model fits worst — it has SF winning Fermanagh and South
    Tyrone by 31% where the truth is 8.9%.
  * What would be needed to do better: 2015, 2017 and 2019 Westminster results,
    which are not in the repo (`data/dz21_to_pc08.csv` already maps the old
    boundaries, so they are usable). That would give roughly 15 pact events over
    ~72 seat-elections. Given the first finding, expect the best predictor to
    come out as "there was a pact here last time" — history, not a model.
* **A party election strategy layer** (owner's idea): every so often, each
  party in turn spends a budget — proportional to its support — across the
  constituencies where it might win, the spend lifting its vote at the expense
  of the parties whose voters transfer to it most readily. It aims at the
  clearest remaining error: the seats we miss are the concentrated-campaign
  ones (North Antrim, South Antrim, Foyle, Belfast South).

  What [the measurement](#campaign-concentration) says about building it:
  * The ceiling is real and bounded, about ×2.2 in share, so the effect size
    has a calibration rather than a guess.
  * The hoped-for elegance is not there. One uniform effect size plus an
    optimiser would produce a competitiveness-shaped pattern, and
    competitiveness explains only 9% of what actually happens. Matching
    reality means per-party ceilings as an input, which is closer to assuming
    the answer.
  * It splits in two: a modest strategic part (×1.2 in contention, ×0.9 for
    also-rans) that an optimiser can genuinely generate, and a much larger
    seat-level lottery — Hanna, Swann, Eastwood — that is candidate-specific,
    unpredictable from position, and already what the Westminster floor
    represents.
  * It would belong to first past the post: the STV comparison gives about
    half the spread. The tactical half of the effect is now
    [built](#tactical-voting); what is left for a strategy layer is the
    campaign concentration.
  * Watch for best-response oscillation if parties re-optimise against each
    other every N steps; one greedy pass in random order avoids it.
  * Saving budget by standing aside is a separate question -- standing is
    driven by pacts and principle too, and the party editor already covers it.

* **Party compatibility from the transfer matrix** (owner's suggestion): rank
  how close parties are by how freely their voters transfer, and use that to
  put a number on abstention or defection after a merger — a merger of two
  parties whose voters barely transfer to each other should leak more than one
  of natural allies. Fun, and fundamentally non-rigorous: the matrix measures
  lower preferences under STV, not whether someone would follow a party into a
  merger.
