What I want to add next is the 'shape sanity' variable.
Essentially regions should be punished for having extremely extended, convoluted shapes, interleaving with other regions etc.
It is important that this is size-independent, and as computationally efficient as possible. Fortunately we only need to calculate for one (two) regions per build (optimise) step.
I'm open to suggestions, but my initial idea to capture the idea of the sshape checker woulfd be conceptually as follows for a region:
    1. Take the shape of the region, and consider its bounding box (a circle would in theory be better but I suspect calculating a true bounding circle is non-trivial computationally).
    2. Conceptually, the box is expanded to a square, with the shorter side of the bounding box lengthened evenly on each side.
    3. Sample eg 10,000 points evenly across the square, with each point having a score proportional to exp(-d/L), for d the distance from the centre of the box, and L the square side length. I'm open to suggestions/improvements about the nature of the scoring.
    4. All points which lie OUTSIDE the region are summed, and value normalised to total (so high score is bad). With an appropriate normalisation constant, which will need to be hand-tuned, this can be added to the overall score function. 
I think this algorithm has the properties of 1. being size independent 2. punishing shapes which are sparsely filling area in general 3. punishing those in particular which do not have a filled centre
Once a sensible default scale is picked, the 'effect strength' for this property should be tunable up or down by the user, inclsuing turning it off entirely (in which case we should be smart enough to not calculate it at all)
